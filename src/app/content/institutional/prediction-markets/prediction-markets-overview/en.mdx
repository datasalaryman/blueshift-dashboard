In the days before the 2024 U.S. presidential election, pollsters called it a toss-up. FiveThirtyEight gave Kamala Harris a 50% chance. Nate Silver's model showed a statistical dead heat.

On Polymarket, Trump led 58% to 42%.

Hours before the Associated Press called the race, [Polymarket showed Trump at 95%](https://www.cnn.com/2024/11/08/business/polymarket-election-trump-nightcap). The platform processed [$9 billion in trading volume](https://www.theblock.co/post/333050/polymarkets-huge-year-9-billion-in-volume-and-314000-active-traders-redefine-prediction-markets) across 2024, with 314,000 active traders wagering real money on outcomes.

This was not a lucky guess. Peer-reviewed research comparing prediction markets to poll aggregators across the 2008, 2012, and 2020 elections found that [betting markets consistently outperformed FiveThirtyEight's forecasts](https://cointelegraph.com/news/prediction-markets-polymarket-polls) in presidential, Senate, House, and governor races.

The difference: prediction markets ask people to bet money on what they think will happen. Polls ask people what they want to happen. One has consequences for being wrong.

<ArticleSection name="What Prediction Markets Are" id="what-prediction-markets-are" level="h2" />

A prediction market is a trading exchange where participants buy and sell shares representing the probability of specific outcomes. Share prices range from $0.01 to $1.00, directly reflecting the market's collective probability estimate.

If a "Yes" share on "Fed cuts rates in January" trades at $0.73, the market believes there's a 73% chance of that outcome. When the event resolves, winning shares pay $1.00. Losing shares pay nothing.

### A Financial Primitive, Not Gambling

The mechanics are identical to binary options and structurally similar to puts and calls. A prediction market share is a contract that pays a fixed amount if a condition is met—the same payoff structure institutions use daily for hedging and speculation.

The difference is what the condition references. Traditional derivatives reference price movements: "SPY above $450 by December expiration." Prediction markets reference real-world events: "Fed cuts rates by January FOMC."

This is why prediction markets are better understood as event derivatives than as betting platforms. The underlying instrument is an event outcome rather than an asset price, but the trading mechanics, risk management, and capital efficiency considerations are familiar territory.

Binary options on interest rate decisions already trade on regulated exchanges. Prediction markets extend the same structure to events that don't yet have liquid derivatives coverage—elections, regulatory approvals, geopolitical developments, corporate milestones.

### Why the Mechanism Works

Traders with better information profit by buying underpriced shares before the market corrects. This creates a financial incentive to do research (unlike polls, where respondents have no stake in accuracy).

Prices update continuously as new information emerges; for this reason manipulation is expensive: moving prices requires capital, and sustaining artificial prices means losing money to arbitrageurs who recognize the mispricing.

<ArticleSection name="The Accuracy Record" id="accuracy-record" level="h2" />

The evidence for prediction market accuracy spans decades and domains.

### Election Forecasting

[Research published in peer-reviewed journals](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0261379412000467) found that prediction markets are more accurate long-run forecasting tools than polls—not just on election eve, but across entire campaign cycles.

The 2024 election provided the most visible demonstration. While major polling aggregators showed a coin flip, prediction markets identified the likely winner with high confidence. The market wasn't reacting to information unavailable to pollsters; it was aggregating that information more effectively.

### Corporate Forecasting

[Google operated internal prediction markets](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/08/the-death-and-life-of-prediction-markets-at-google) for product launches, hiring, and other business metrics. Thousands of employees forecast hundreds of events. The markets consistently delivered accurate predictions about strategic outcomes.

[Hewlett-Packard tested prediction markets](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/dQhjwHA7LhfE8YpYF/prediction-markets-in-the-corporate-setting) for forecasting computer workstation sales from 1996 to 1999. In six of eight cases, the internal market outperformed HP's official corporate forecasts.

Microsoft, Best Buy, and other Fortune 500 companies have experimented with internal prediction markets for budget management, make-or-buy decisions, and strategic planning.

### Scientific Replication

In a global study on scientific replication, [market participants correctly predicted which behavioral-science experiments would replicate](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/prediction-markets-for-economic-forecasting/) at approximately 73% accuracy—substantially better than traditional expert surveys.

Financial incentives tied to accuracy, combined with price aggregation across diverse perspectives, consistently outperform conventional forecasting.

<ArticleSection name="Why On-Chain Infrastructure Matters" id="why-on-chain" level="h2" />

Prediction markets existed for decades before anyone could trade them at scale. The Iowa Electronic Markets launched in 1988. Intrade operated through the 2000s. PredictIt began in 2014.

The concept was never the problem. Access to liquidity was.

### The Limits of Traditional Infrastructure

[PredictIt operated under a CFTC no-action letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PredictIt) that imposed severe restrictions: $850 maximum position per contract, 5,000 traders per market, and limited market types. These constraints existed because U.S. regulators classified larger prediction markets as gambling or unregistered securities.

The $850 cap meant institutional participation was impossible. Markets remained thin, with limited liquidity and wide spreads. Academic researchers used the data, but serious capital could not participate.

Intrade, which allowed larger positions, was shut down entirely in 2012 after CFTC enforcement action. When it collapsed, users lost funds trapped on the platform; a counterparty risk that traditional prediction markets could never fully eliminate.

On-chain prediction markets solve specific structural problems that limited their predecessors.

### Counterparty Risk Elimination

When you trade on a traditional prediction market, you trust the platform to:
- Hold your funds safely
- Execute settlements correctly
- Remain solvent until your positions resolve
- Not abscond with deposits

Intrade's collapse demonstrated what happens when that trust is misplaced. Users had no recourse.

[On-chain prediction markets operate differently](https://docs.polymarket.com/). Smart contracts hold funds in escrow and execute settlement automatically when oracles report outcomes. The contract is the counterparty. Settlement is guaranteed by code, not by a company's balance sheet or operational integrity.

For institutions, this changes the risk calculus. Due diligence shifts from evaluating platform solvency to auditing smart contract code; a one-time assessment rather than ongoing counterparty monitoring.

### Global Liquidity Aggregation

Traditional prediction markets fragment liquidity by jurisdiction. U.S. users trade on PredictIt or Kalshi. European users access different platforms. Asian markets are separate entirely. Each pool is shallow; spreads are wide; price discovery suffers.

On-chain markets aggregate global capital into unified liquidity pools. A trader in Singapore and a trader in London provide liquidity to the same order book, tightening spreads and improving execution for everyone.

[Polymarket processed $9 billion in volume across 2024](https://www.theblock.co/post/333050/polymarkets-huge-year-9-billion-in-volume-and-314000-active-traders-redefine-prediction-markets); approximately 50x what PredictIt could handle in an entire year under its regulatory constraints. [$2.63 billion traded in November 2024 alone](https://www.theblock.co/post/333050/polymarkets-huge-year-9-billion-in-volume-and-314000-active-traders-redefine-prediction-markets). This liquidity depth enables institutional-size positions without moving markets.

### Transparent and Auditable Markets

Every trade, position, and settlement on an on-chain prediction market is publicly recorded. Institutions can:

- **Verify execution quality.** Compare your fill prices against the full order book history.
- **Audit settlement accuracy.** Confirm that payouts matched oracle reports.
- **Detect manipulation attempts.** Large position changes are visible in real time.
- **Assess market depth.** Evaluate liquidity before committing capital.

Traditional platforms provide whatever transparency they choose to offer. On-chain markets provide transparency by default—the blockchain is the audit trail.

### Composability with Financial Infrastructure

A position on PredictIt is a number in a database. A position on an on-chain prediction market is a token, a programmable financial instrument.

On-chain positions can be used as collateral, transferred before resolution, or integrated into automated strategies. [Drift Protocol's BET platform](https://www.drift.trade/updates/introducing-bet-the-first-capital-efficient-prediction-market-on-solana) accepted 30+ tokens as collateral, including yield-bearing assets, and let traders combine prediction market positions with perpetual futures hedges in unified portfolios.

### Permissionless Market Creation

Traditional prediction markets offer whatever events the platform chooses to list. PredictIt focused on U.S. politics. Kalshi offers CFTC-approved contract types. If the event you care about isn't listed, you cannot trade it.

On-chain infrastructure enables permissionless market creation. Anyone can create a market on any verifiable event. This means:

- **Niche coverage.** Markets on sector-specific regulatory decisions, corporate milestones, or regional political outcomes that mass-market platforms would never list.
- **Faster listing.** New markets can launch in minutes, not after weeks of platform review.
- **Custom resolution criteria.** Market creators define precisely what constitutes each outcome.

For institutions with specific forecasting needs, permissionless creation means prediction market coverage of events that actually matter to their portfolios.

### Infrastructure Permanence

Traditional prediction markets can be shut down. Intrade was. PredictIt nearly was, spending years in litigation after the CFTC revoked its no-action letter in 2022. [The platform only survived through a Fifth Circuit ruling](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/07/16/3116432/0/en/PredictIt-Announces-Regulatory-Agreement-Supporting-Broader-Public-Participation.html) that found the CFTC's action "arbitrary and capricious."

On-chain markets operate on distributed infrastructure. Smart contracts execute as long as the underlying blockchain runs. No single entity can unilaterally shut down the market or freeze user funds.

For institutions building systems around prediction market data, this permanence matters. Integrating prediction market signals into risk models or trading strategies requires confidence that the data source will continue operating.

<ArticleSection name="Institutional Applications" id="institutional-applications" level="h2" />

[Hedge funds are increasingly evaluating prediction markets](https://www.valuewalk.com/why-hedge-funds-are-eyeing-prediction-markets-in-2025-six-key-drivers/) as both signal sources and trading venues. The applications fall into three categories.

### Alternative Data for Forecasting

Prediction market prices provide real-time probability estimates that complement traditional analysis:

- **Macro forecasting.** Markets on Fed rate decisions, inflation prints, and GDP releases update continuously as new information emerges.

- **Geopolitical risk.** Election outcomes, policy changes, and international developments priced in real time across global markets.

- **Sector-specific events.** Regulatory approvals, merger completions, and product launches—events that move asset prices—have corresponding prediction markets.

Traditional forecasting relies on models updated periodically. Prediction markets provide continuous price signals that reflect the latest information from diverse participants.

### Hedging Event Risk

Prediction markets enable direct exposure to event outcomes. [The ability to hedge specific risks creates market liquidity](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-11851-2_9) that traditional derivatives cannot match.

Consider a fund with significant exposure to pharmaceutical stocks. A prediction market on FDA approval outcomes allows direct hedging of regulatory risk—more precise than sector puts, more cost-effective than holding cash.

Or a fund with assets sensitive to U.S. trade policy. Markets on tariff implementation or trade agreement ratification provide hedging instruments that don't exist in traditional derivatives markets.

### Alpha Generation

[Institutional capital remains largely absent from prediction markets](https://www.valuewalk.com/why-hedge-funds-are-eyeing-prediction-markets-in-2025-six-key-drivers/), which means mispricings persist longer than they would in more efficient markets. Funds with strong research capabilities can identify contracts where prices diverge from fundamental probability estimates. These inefficiencies narrow as more capital enters.

<ArticleSection name="The Regulatory Trajectory" id="regulatory-trajectory" level="h2" />

The regulatory environment for prediction markets has shifted materially in 2025.

[The CFTC issued no-action letters to Polymarket, PredictIt, and other platforms](https://cointelegraph.com/news/cftc-prediction-markets-event-contracts-relief) in December 2025, reducing recordkeeping requirements and clarifying operational pathways. Congressional testimony has highlighted growing interest in formalizing prediction markets within financial regulatory frameworks.

[DLA Piper convened hedge fund executives](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/events/2025/12/prediction-markets-what-hedge-funds-need-to-know) in December 2025 to address prediction market legal and operational considerations. The legal infrastructure for institutional participation is developing alongside the trading infrastructure.

<ArticleSection name="Conclusion" id="conclusion" level="h2" />

Prediction markets have outperformed polls and expert forecasts across elections, corporate planning, and scientific replication studies. The mechanism, financial incentives tied to accuracy, produces better probability estimates than alternatives that lack skin in the game.

On-chain infrastructure removed the liquidity constraints that limited earlier prediction markets. Polymarket processed $9 billion in 2024. Settlement is trustless. Markets run continuously.

For institutions, the applications are straightforward: alternative data for forecasting, direct hedging of event risk, and trading on mispricings in still-inefficient markets. The infrastructure exists on both regulated U.S. platforms and permissionless global markets.
